## topic-07 — Protect your main email account without locking yourself out

**Reader hook:** Your phone is gone. Can you still get into the email account that resets your other passwords?

**Problem and possible benefit:** A reader may strengthen sign-in while leaving recovery dependent on the same device. Investigate how to make one important personal email account harder to take over while retaining an accessible recovery route. The useful outcome is one account checked and a recovery dependency understood, not a promise of complete security.

**Readership and applicability:** Mature teenagers through older adults, including people helping someone with permission. Advice concerns personal accounts the reader controls. Managed school/work accounts, existing compromise, coercive control and targeted high-risk situations need different support; this is not an incident-response guide. Provider, account type and available devices matter.

**Approaches to compare during research:** Recovery preparation before changing sign-in; passkeys where suitable; a unique password and an appropriate second factor where needed; trusted-device security and updates; recognising deceptive sign-in requests. Compare overlap and dependencies before selecting the eventual foundations. Do not treat passkeys, passwords and MFA as three independent actions everyone must add.

**Evidence screen:**

- [NCSC: Passkeys](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/passkeys). **Relevant guidance inspected:** definition, security rationale, credential-manager handling and alternatives when unavailable. Supports comparing passkeys with password/2SV options. This is agency guidance, not a measured estimate of an individual reader's risk reduction. Its general description of synchronisation is not proof that every passkey or recovery arrangement behaves identically.
- [Google: Fix common issues with 2-Step Verification](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/185834). **Provider guidance inspected:** lost phone, backup options, lost security key and unavailable passkey. Lists previously configured alternatives and account recovery routes. Access initially timed out; one retry succeeded. It does not guarantee recovery or establish another provider's behaviour. Special protection programmes have different conditions.
- [Microsoft: Help with the account recovery form](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accounts-billing/manage/help-with-the-microsoft-account-recovery-form). **Provider guidance inspected:** the important warning and recovery prerequisites. It states that the form/support cannot bypass two-step verification when all alternate verification methods are unavailable. This makes recovery preparation material; it is not a universal statement about every recovery-code route.
- [Microsoft: Account recovery code](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accounts-billing/manage/how-to-get-a-microsoft-account-recovery-code). **Provider guidance inspected:** creating/storing a code, using it and the two-step-verification waiting-period qualification. Supports researching a provider-specific contingency. No actual code was created or used.

**Duplication and exclusions:** No overlap with the three published subjects. Topic-14 shares account-access dependencies but its primary outcome is recovering file bytes. Keep this article focused on account access; do not expand it into backups, every scam or a catalogue of security products. No paid security key or password-manager purchase is assumed.

**Open research before drafting:** Check current passkey and recovery instructions for the providers actually used as examples; distinguish synced and device-bound credentials; check recovery-contact/code storage and dependencies; explain a safe check without disabling working sign-in methods or exposing codes. Validate any phishing-resistance wording without implying immunity to all fraud. Determine whether the scope supports a clear three-idea guide without a dense provider matrix.

## topic-14 — Could you recover your photos if your phone disappeared?

**Reader hook:** Your photos appear on two devices. Would they survive the same deletion?

**Problem and possible benefit:** Seeing a library elsewhere may protect against losing one phone while leaving accidental deletion or account loss unresolved. Help readers identify what is protected, what remains dependent on one service, and how to check a harmless sample copy without risking originals.

**Readership and applicability:** People storing their own valued photos or helping with permission, with no technical expertise or paid storage assumed. Use ordinary consumer libraries as examples. Exclude forensic recovery, repair of damaged media, business retention, bypassing another person's account and guarantees that an entire library is recoverable.

**Approaches to compare during research:** Check upload completion and account access; distinguish synchronisation from an independent copy; retain originals in another suitable place; check the contents and readability of a sample; understand deletion recovery. Compare which protect against loss, deletion and account lockout rather than forcing a slogan into three recommendations.

**Evidence screen:**

- [NCSC: Backing up your data](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/small-organisations-guide-to-cyber-security/backing-up-your-data). **Relevant guidance inspected:** online storage, separate storage devices, combined copies and restoring backups. Supports checking restoration and keeping copies with different failure modes. It is written for small organisations but links to consumer instructions; it is a research route, not a complete consumer photo workflow.
- [Apple: Set up and use iCloud Photos](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/108782). **Provider guidance inspected:** operation, changes/deletion, downloading copies and optimised storage. Describes deletion propagating across iCloud Photos devices, a 30-day Recently Deleted window and photos not being duplicated in iCloud Backup. An initial rate limit cleared on one retry. This is documentation, not an account or restore test; shared libraries and other services need separate checks.
- [Google Photos: Delete photos and videos](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6128858?hl=en). **Provider guidance inspected:** before deletion, deletion effects, trash and permanent deletion. Describes deletion affecting backed-up devices and states a 30-day trash window in the retrieved current page. Do not import a remembered retention period or assume every client/account behaves identically. Confirm the exact applicable instructions again before article review; no live deletion was performed.

**Duplication and exclusions:** Distinct from the published study-memory article: this concerns file recovery, not human memory. Account protection overlaps topic-07 only as a prerequisite. Keep it secondary rather than writing two versions of the email-security article. Do not recommend deleting an original to demonstrate recovery, signing out of a working account or buying storage without assessing need.

**Open research before drafting:** Inspect official export/download and restore instructions for the selected two services; distinguish originals from optimised previews, sync folders from independent copies, and sample success from whole-library coverage. Check storage capacity, recurring maintenance and access after losing a device. A safe sample must preserve the source and avoid private material in screenshots. Recovery windows are not substitutes for another copy. The current screen has not yet established a complete practical sequence.

## topic-15 — Keep leftovers useful, not forgotten

**Reader hook:** The spare portion was meant to become lunch. What needs to happen before you forget it?

**Problem and possible benefit:** Investigate a small routine that turns ordinary home-cooked leftovers into a planned meal while preserving food safety. The benefit is less uncertainty and a usable next action, not a quantified saving or a claim that reheating rescues badly stored food.

**Readership and applicability:** Mature teenagers and adults preparing or storing household food, including people cooking for others. Use explicitly identified UK household guidance as the initial evidence base; an international readership does not make national time limits universal. Higher-risk people, food-specific requirements, packaging and uncertain prior handling need visible qualifications.

**Approaches to compare during research:** Portioning/cooling and refrigeration; deciding when to eat or freeze; labelling and visibility; safe thawing/reheating; avoiding surplus at cooking. Safety may constrain the routine rather than compete as an optional tip. Do not choose three final recommendations before comparing these alternatives.

**Evidence screen:**

- [FSA: How to chill, freeze and defrost food safely](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/how-to-chill-freeze-and-defrost-food-safely/how-to-chill-freeze-and-defrost-food-safely). **Relevant guidance inspected:** applicability, headline recommendations and chilling. Specifies England, Northern Ireland and Wales; discusses smaller portions, prompt refrigeration and using or freezing leftovers within its stated limits. Published 18 December 2017; accessed today. Guidance is not comparative research proving the best three actions or an international standard.
- [Food Standards Scotland: Cooking](https://www.foodstandards.gov.scot/consumer-advice/food-safety/food-safety-in-the-kitchen/cooking). **Relevant guidance inspected:** leftovers and takeaway rice. Covers cooling, refrigerated storage, thorough reheating and reheating only once; distinguishes rice and uncertain takeaway handling. It supplies Scottish guidance rather than evidence about all jurisdictions.
- [Food Standards Scotland: Food storage](https://www.foodstandards.gov.scot/consumer-advice/food-safety/food-safety-in-the-kitchen/food-storage). **Relevant guidance inspected:** labels and rice. Explains the spore/toxin concern, faster rice cooling and its shorter refrigerated storage limit. Supports a food-specific exception; no microbiology paper was inspected.

**Duplication and exclusions:** No published food-storage article or open draft was found. Topic-08 concerns dietary choices, not leftovers; topic-03 concerns financial resilience, so do not recast this as another budgeting article. Exclude catering, long power failures, canning and unverified instructions for every food type. Smell is not proposed as a safety test.

**Open research before drafting:** Inspect freezing, defrosting and reheating instructions fully; check guidance for higher-risk people and material food exceptions. Decide whether explicit UK scope is useful enough or a second national source is needed for international comparison. Avoid mixing incompatible cooling instructions or presenting one national limit as universal. Verify any practical labelling/visibility suggestion separately from claims of measured waste reduction. Keep necessary safety in the same reading layer as the advice it qualifies.
