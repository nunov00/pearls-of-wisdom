## Make room for sleep without chasing a perfect night (topic-04)

- **Reader hook:** What can you change when sleep advice itself starts to feel like another task?
- **Problem:** Identify manageable changes for occasional sleep difficulty and recognise when self-help is insufficient.
- **Readers and applicability:** Mature-teen and adult readers, including people supporting someone else. Develop occasional sleep difficulty as the problem; verify age-appropriate habits and sleep needs before advice is applied by a teenager. Adult evidence does not automatically establish teen applicability; shift work and persistent insomnia need separate handling.
- **Possible angle:** Compare routine, sleep opportunity and relevant environmental or stimulant changes. Separate general habits from clinical treatment.
- **Potential benefit:** A realistic first experiment rather than a rigid optimisation checklist.
- **Evidence lead:** [Source](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/). Inspected: Symptoms; causes; self-help; when to seek help, 28 October 2026. NHS guidance identifies everyday habits and possible underlying conditions. Research on treatments and competing foundations still needs checking.
- **Limits and checks:** Do not offer sleep restriction as casual self-help, promise a cure, or hide routes for persistent symptoms or interrupted breathing.
- **What would improve it:** Narrow the article further and verify referral boundaries and which actions deserve priority.

- **Reviewed disposition:** Develop with narrowed scope. Claude's review covers the initial commit; this amended brief has not been re-reviewed.

## Finish one important task in a day full of interruptions (topic-05)

- **Reader hook:** You were busy all day. Why is the important task still waiting?
- **Problem:** Create a realistic opportunity to progress one task despite interruptions.
- **Readers and applicability:** People with some control over their work or study; carers, front-line staff and urgent-response roles may have little control.
- **Possible angle:** Compare defining a next action, protecting a feasible work period and managing notification or checking habits. Treat this as a practical hypothesis, not a proven three-part recipe.
- **Potential benefit:** A bounded experiment readers can judge against their actual responsibilities.
- **Evidence lead:** [Source](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/notifications-and-awareness-a-field-study-of-alert-usage-and-preferences/). Inspected: Research abstract only; full paper not inspected, 28 October 2026. A 2010 workplace field-study abstract reports differing responses to removing alerts. It is a caution against universal advice, not sufficient evidence for the proposed routine.
- **Limits and checks:** Do not blame people for structural workload or claim that disabling notifications always improves focus.
- **What would improve it:** Find stronger and more recent primary research for the specific interventions before selecting foundations.

- **Reviewed disposition:** Revise evidence before commissioning. Claude's review covers the initial commit; this amended brief has not been re-reviewed.

## Protect your main email account without locking yourself out (topic-07)

- **Reader hook:** If you lost access to your main email tomorrow, could you get back in safely?
- **Problem:** Secure a main email account while keeping a workable recovery route.
- **Readers and applicability:** People managing their own accounts or helping someone with permission; settings and recovery depend on the provider.
- **Possible angle:** Compare passkeys where supported, strong unique passwords with two-step verification where needed, and recovery preparation. These are candidate foundations, not an approved final list.
- **Potential benefit:** A concrete, bounded account-security task rather than a tour of every security feature.
- **Evidence lead:** [Source](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/passkeys). Inspected: What is a passkey?; If passkeys aren't available, 28 October 2026. Official guidance supports investigating passkeys and password/2SV alternatives; it does not verify individual providers' recovery behaviour.
- **Limits and checks:** Do not imply phishing resistance prevents all scams or promise every passkey is synced. Verify provider-specific recovery before advising changes.
- **What would improve it:** Check recovery guidance for representative providers and find a clearer opening that earns 9 for appeal.

- **Reviewed disposition:** Develop. Claude's review covers the initial commit; this amended brief has not been re-reviewed.

## Improve everyday meals with changes you can keep (topic-08)

- **Reader hook:** What could you change in meals you already eat, without starting a new diet?
- **Problem:** Find affordable, culturally adaptable food changes within an existing routine.
- **Readers and applicability:** Mature-teen and adult readers choosing meals for themselves or helping someone else. Verify age-appropriate examples and nutritional needs; adult targets must not be transferred to teenagers. Clinical diets, allergies and eating disorders require explicit boundaries.
- **Possible angle:** Compare practical substitutions and additions, including fibre-rich staples and food variety; do not equate all processed foods or invent a measured 80/20 benefit.
- **Potential benefit:** Examples based on ordinary food, time and budget rather than supplements or perfection.
- **Evidence lead:** [Source](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet). Inspected: Overview; carbohydrates; foods, 28 October 2026. WHO population guidance provides a starting framework, with individual and cultural variation. The three most useful practical changes are not established by this screen.
- **Limits and checks:** Keep general population guidance distinct from personalised treatment; avoid moralising and unsupported weight-loss promises.
- **What would improve it:** Replace the generic healthy-eating promise with a distinctive everyday choice and check evidence for the actual substitutions.
- **Reviewed disposition:** Park until a distinctive angle exists. Claude's review covers the initial commit; this amended brief has not been re-reviewed.

## Cut avoidable home energy use without making life harder (topic-12)

- **Reader hook:** Which small changes are worth your effort in the home you actually have?
- **Problem:** Find low-cost changes relevant to a household's real energy uses.
- **Readers and applicability:** Renters and owners in varied homes and climates; control of appliances and heating differs.
- **Possible angle:** Investigate appliance use, hot-water use and avoidable heat loss; compare effort and applicability before choosing three.
- **Potential benefit:** An adaptable decision guide rather than a list of UK cash-saving estimates.
- **Evidence lead:** [Source](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/advice/draught-proofing/). Inspected: Ventilation; what not to block, 28 October 2026. Direct guidance distinguishes unwanted draughts from necessary ventilation. Other energy uses and cross-country economics need additional sources.
- **Limits and checks:** Preserve ventilation, warmth and appliance safety. No universal savings figures, unsafe hot-water settings or blocking essential vents.
- **What would improve it:** Find a stronger hook and sources that support comparing actions across homes, rather than repeating an advice list.

- **Reviewed disposition:** Park/revise scope and evidence. Claude's review covers the initial commit; this amended brief has not been re-reviewed.

## A daily routine that protects your teeth and gums (topic-13)

- **Reader hook:** Could rinsing after brushing undo part of what your toothpaste is meant to do?
- **Problem:** Choose useful daily oral-care actions without buying an elaborate collection of products.
- **Readers and applicability:** People caring for adult teeth; children's fluoride amounts, dentures and individual dental conditions need separate guidance.
- **Possible angle:** Investigate fluoride brushing, cleaning between teeth and dietary sugar exposure. Compare these with check-ups; do not force three independent or universally ranked recommendations.
- **Potential benefit:** A familiar routine with specific decisions a reader can check today.
- **Evidence lead:** [Source](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-teeth-and-gums/how-to-keep-your-teeth-clean/). Inspected: Toothpaste; brushing; cleaning between teeth, 28 October 2026. Public guidance gives a practical starting point on fluoride and cleaning. It does not establish a comparative ranking of all candidate actions.
- **Limits and checks:** Prevention is not treatment for pain, persistent bleeding or infection. Seek exact evidence for each foundation and distinguish tooth decay from gum disease.
- **What would improve it:** Build around the NHS guidance on not rinsing immediately after brushing; avoid a familiar brush-your-teeth recap and verify dietary and professional-care alternatives.

- **Reviewed disposition:** Revise hook around a useful misconception. Claude's review covers the initial commit; this amended brief has not been re-reviewed.

## Could you recover your photos if your phone disappeared? (topic-14)

- **Reader hook:** Seeing a photo on a second device is reassuring. Could you recover it after a mistake?
- **Problem:** Check that valued personal files can actually be recovered after loss or deletion.
- **Readers and applicability:** People storing personal photos or documents; no assumed technical expertise or paid storage plan.
- **Possible angle:** Identify what matters, investigate an independent recoverable copy and practise restoring a harmless sample. Verify each platform's sync, retention and deletion behaviour.
- **Potential benefit:** A concrete recovery check rather than a promise that a cloud logo means everything is protected.
- **Evidence lead:** [Source](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/small-organisations-guide-to-cyber-security/backing-up-your-data). Inspected: Storage-device backups; restoring backups, 28 October 2026. NCSC guidance discusses separate copies and checking restoration. Although written for small organisations, it links directly to consumer backup and restore guides. This is a credible consumer research route; those linked guides and service-specific sync, retention and deletion behaviour still require verification.
- **Limits and checks:** Do not delete originals for a demonstration, expose private files or recommend storage purchases without need. Account security and recovery overlap with topic-07 but file restoration is a different outcome.
- **What would improve it:** Verify two common consumer services and use a safe example understandable without technical terminology.

- **Reviewed disposition:** Develop; verify consumer service behaviour. Claude's review covers the initial commit; this amended brief has not been re-reviewed.

## Keep leftovers useful, not forgotten (topic-15)

- **Reader hook:** Dinner is over. What happens to the spare portions next?
- **Problem:** Make a repeatable plan for cooling, storing and using cooked leftovers.
- **Readers and applicability:** People preparing or storing meals; start with ordinary cooked leftovers and explicitly handle food-specific exceptions.
- **Possible angle:** Explore prompt safe cooling, clear storage planning and safe use or freezing. Do not turn these tentative stages into universal instructions.
- **Potential benefit:** A small household routine with a visible next action and less guesswork.
- **Evidence lead:** [Source](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/how-to-chill-freeze-and-defrost-food-safely/how-to-chill-freeze-and-defrost-food-safely). Inspected: Chilling food; freezing food, 28 October 2026. FSA guidance covers chilling and freezing; it is labelled for England, Northern Ireland and Wales. International applicability and food-specific exceptions remain open.
- **Limits and checks:** Food-specific limits, vulnerable groups, use-by dates and reheating need checked guidance. Do not use smell as proof of safety or universalise a national storage rule.
- **What would improve it:** Check Food Standards Scotland as well as rice and other exceptions and decide how much fits without burying essential safety.

- **Reviewed disposition:** Develop; check food-specific exceptions and Scottish guidance. Claude's review covers the initial commit; this amended brief has not been re-reviewed.
