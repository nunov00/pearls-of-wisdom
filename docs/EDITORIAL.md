# Editorial standard

Define a practical goal, intended readers, the people or situations the guidance concerns, and assumptions. Choose three strong foundations by likely impact (40%), evidence (30%), applicability (20%) and independence (10%). These are internal heuristics, not scientific scores. Record rejected alternatives. Add an essential recommendation when needed, with a qualitative rationale; the approximate 20% guideline is not a measured outcome share. Do not force an unsupported universal rank.

Each recommendation states what to do, why, a proportionate consequence of omission, and a realistic first step. Explain benefits without inventing a separate harm. The collapsed overview should be understandable in about five seconds: three ideas with one short benefit each. The expanded guide supplies the practical reading; essential safety stays visible beside the advice it qualifies.

Use British English and concrete language. Sources must support the exact material claim. Distinguish guidelines, causal evidence and associations. Strong/moderate/limited/consensus-based labels are informal editorial judgements with reasons, not formal GRADE ratings. Publisher reputation alone does not establish certainty.

Record source URL, checked date, inspected section, support and limits. Reading a publisher summary does not mean the full paper was checked. Link-check success is not factual verification. Do not publish a material claim that depends on inaccessible, unverified evidence without accessible substantiation.

Independent review: first consider plausible leading recommendations without seeing the writer’s rationale; then inspect the draft, exclusions, sources, consequences, scope and accessibility. Record blocker/important/minor findings. Blockers and important findings prevent publication; minor polish does not. Allow an initial article review plus no more than three substantive correction rounds (up to four reviews), then return work that remains below the publication standard to backlog with its findings. Topic corrections retain their separate limit in IDEAS.md. A changed article hash needs updated validation. Never label self-review independent.

Preserve original dates on revision. Show the latest meaningful revised date. Return weak articles to backlog; withdraw materially misleading advice immediately with a reason. The queue combines revisions and new work; urgent corrections take precedence, otherwise choose by merit.

Health and finance are general education: visible audience and exceptions, no personalised recommendations. Financial guidance must identify jurisdiction assumptions; debt priority is not determined solely by interest rate. Medical contraindications must not be hidden in expansions. A disclaimer cannot make unsafe content acceptable.

Initial articles: exercise after 65, remembering study material, financial breathing room. Sources, recommendation selection and minor corrections have independent same-provider reviews in docs/reviews/. Learning explicitly presents a connected study routine because feedback overlaps retrieval. Final dated article bytes require matching release records; review completion is not proof of deployment.

## Reader experience and visual review

Owner-approved refinement, 27 October 2026: judge the page as an inviting entry point with optional depth. Do not fill the overview with numerical targets, duplicate summaries, audience small print or general warning blocks. A summary must remain accurate on its own; retain any exception needed to avoid a misleading or unsafe claim in that same reading layer. Detailed advice includes what to do, why, a realistic first step, its limits and checked references.

Before considering an article ready, inspect the rendered page on a phone and desktop, both collapsed and expanded. Content checks alone do not establish visual quality. Use this checklist alongside factual review:

- Three distinct ideas are understandable at a glance, with consistent names in the overview and guide. Avoid repeating the same list or title as another summary.
- Illustrations form a coherent, contemporary set, read clearly at phone size and fit the page background without visible boxes or accidental cropping. They must represent the idea without implying unsafe technique.
- Owner direction, 31 October 2026: every new article has one main image (`mainImage`) above its title. The three idea cards are text-only, retaining their tinted backgrounds and readable headings/benefits; do not generate or display an image or decorative number for every idea. The main image provides the visual opening and shared-link picture. Additional illustrations belong in the expanded guide only when they explain something useful; they are optional, not a quota. Archived overview image metadata/assets may remain for historical records but are not rendered by the shared overview template.
- Supply the main page image (`src`, 1200×630, WebP) and a matching JPEG or PNG share copy (`shareSrc`, 1200×630), with descriptive `alt` text. Keep people and key objects in the central area because apps crop share pictures differently; keep words out of generated artwork. Choose colours for the article's subject rather than reusing cobalt by default. Any explanatory guide illustration should be coherent with that article, without requiring identical scenes or repeating its cover.
- Main images are required from the 1–6 November trial article onwards; a new article without one is not ready for publication. The reviewer records the SHA-256 of both main-image files' raw bytes and any other article-specific illustration in the exact review manifest. The release check hashes the article YAML only: it catches changed image paths but not replacement bytes at the same path. Any image-byte change therefore requires renewed review. `validate-content` checks that main-image files exist and are 1200×630.
- Keep readable contrast and generous spacing: article body text around 20px, overview text at least 19px, and supporting text at least 15px. Check narrow phones at 320px and browser zoom; do not shrink text to force a layout.
- Use the shared typography, currently locally hosted Source Serif 4 headings and Source Sans 3 body text with readable fallbacks. A new typeface needs visual comparison, not an assumption that a different font is better.
- Give expansion one clear primary control with a concrete benefit, currently “See how to start”. Keep Share secondary. Opening, closing, reference links and returning to the overview must work with keyboard input and without JavaScript.
- Balance title lines, keep navigation tidy, and check the expanded guide for overflow. Keep small numbered references attached to the preceding word without changing line spacing, and retain usable reference links and backlinks.

Record what was actually checked and unresolved findings. Fix confusing reading order, unreadable text and broken controls before publication; visual polish alone is not evidence of factual quality. These criteria underpin the 9+ quality target below; a score must be justified by the actual article and checks performed. Existing source verification, independent review, exact-content validation and publication safeguards still apply.

## Main-image quality and production

Owner direction, 31 October 2026: images must be outstanding enough to attract interest; technical validity and an attractive article layout alone are insufficient. These requirements apply to future drafting/review and meaningful image revisions. Existing approved images are reference examples, not automatic exemptions for future images. A written rule cannot guarantee a great generated result or a reader's response; the actual selected artwork must pass inspection.

### Brief before generation

State the reader's question, the one idea/emotion the cover should convey, the focal subject/action, the composition and subject-appropriate palette. Start from an article-specific visual concept rather than a generic stock scene. A person at a desk is suitable only when the action/props clearly distinguish this topic; do not repeat a successful composition across unrelated subjects by habit. Avoid decorative brains, robots, lightbulbs, floating icons and finance-growth clichés unless they serve a specific, accurate explanation. The accepted main images show the expected finish and warmth, not a mandatory blue-shirt palette or a template to trace.

### What earns a strong visual assessment

- **Interest and meaning:** one immediate focal point, an inviting or intriguing scene, and a clear relationship to this article. It should earn attention when seen with the title at small share-preview size, not merely decorate the page.
- **Craft:** deliberate framing, convincing light, coherent colour, clean anatomy and object geometry, plausible contact/poses, and no distracting artefacts, invented text, watermarks or accidental crop. Inspect faces, hands and key props at full size.
- **Truthfulness:** no unsafe technique, unsupported causal claim, exaggerated medical benefit or promise of financial success. Match the people/situation to the guidance without treating the evidence population as the entire site's readership.
- **Use in context:** inspect the selected 1200×630 export in the actual article at 320/390px and desktop widths, light and dark mode, and as a small landscape preview. Check that a centred square crop still retains the main subject; a real messaging-service scrape is a separate check and must not be claimed from a local mock-up. The JPEG/PNG share copy must show the same approved composition as the WebP.
- **Distinctiveness:** compare with recent covers so adjacent articles do not look interchangeable. Keep any optional explanatory guide images consistent with the article while using them to add information.

### Selection, refinement and limits

Use the built-in image-generation tool within existing subscriptions; no paid API, new subscription or metered service is authorised. Begin with one carefully briefed candidate. If it is weak or generic, try one genuinely different composition before repeatedly polishing it. Select the stronger direction and allow up to two targeted refinements: a normal maximum of four successful generation calls for the main image across the whole article cycle, including correction rounds. An already strong candidate may finish early; an existing accepted image may be deliberately reused after checking its suitability. Do not spend the allowance merely because it exists.

Record candidate/attempt count, what changed, why the final image wins and the actual limits encountered in the PR/review notes. The count does not reset per task or review. A transient failure with no image output may be retried once after inspecting state; it does not justify an unbounded retry loop. Platform limits and the authorised task window may stop work earlier. Never add scheduled sessions, widen the trial, incur costs or lower the visual standard to finish. If the image remains below standard after the bounded attempts, defer it with concrete findings for manual planning; it is not publication-ready.

### Explicit visual review

The author and Claude each record a separate main-image quality score with concise reasons, remaining weaknesses and actual inspection checks, alongside the whole-article score. Both visual scores must be at least 9/10 on the exact selected image bytes before publication; a whole-article 9 cannot compensate for a weak cover. This is a manual editorial quality criterion, not an automated rating or a prediction of engagement. Never round 8.5 up, average the scores or infer independent approval from a self-rating. If either image or article fails its existing quality/evidence/readiness requirements, revise within the same authorised limits or defer. Preserve all existing exact-byte, review-budget and publication safeguards.

## Readers and people the guidance concerns

Owner clarification, 27 October 2026: Pearls of Wisdom is for mature teenagers through to older adults. Readers may apply an idea themselves or use it to support family, friends or someone they care for. Never infer a site-wide reader age from an article's subject or evidence population.

Distinguish who reads an article from who its advice applies to. Exercise guidance based on recommendations for adults aged 65+ may be read by younger relatives or friends helping an older person. Retain the exact applicability and evidence limits of that guidance without assuming the reader is 65+. The current article audience field describes applicability; it is not a restriction on readership. Adult-scoped advice is not automatically advice for teenagers.

Write accessible, respectful language across ages. Use a natural range of ages in illustrations across publications; the pictured person's age need not match every reader. Readable typography benefits everyone. Reviewer handoffs must carry this distinction explicitly. Named sections provide structure in the expanded guide; omit decorative 01–03 labels. Keep numbered source references and their links.


## Quality target: 9/10 or higher

Owner direction, 27 October 2026: “The target for all should be 9+, so let’s aim for that.” Apply this target to every new article, meaningful revision and reference pilot. Assess the whole article, including both the opening and expanded guide. A strong opening alone does not establish a 9+ result.

The author self-reviews, then a separate reviewer records an overall score out of ten with concise reasons, remaining weaknesses and the checks actually performed. Publication requires BOTH the author and independent reviewer to score at least 9 on the same exact final article and illustration bytes. The required Pearls review pair is Codex author and Claude independent reviewer, including manual work after the trial unless the Owner explicitly changes that pair. Do not average the scores; a high score from one cannot compensate for a below-9 score from the other. In the review report or PR, address:

- Accuracy and evidence: material claims match checked sources, with honest scope, applicability and uncertainty.
- Clarity and usefulness: plain language, three clear ideas, practical first steps and relevant examples for the broad readership, including people helping others.
- Reading experience: an inviting brief opening and an expanded guide that earns the reader's time, with no unnecessary repetition or research jargon in the main prose.
- Presentation and access: coherent illustrations, readable typography and contrast, phone/desktop layout, usable controls and references in both reading layers.

A 9 means strong across these areas, with only minor polish remaining. Below 9, identify concrete improvements, revise and re-review within the existing correction-round limit; defer work that still falls short rather than lower the target. Do not round 8.5 up to 9, inflate scores after cosmetic edits or average away a significant weakness. Ratings are reasoned editorial judgements, not scientific measurements or a guarantee of reader response.

Accuracy, safety, applicability, readability and functional blockers must be resolved regardless of the overall score. The target supplements existing independent source/content review, exact-byte release validation and publication checks. It is a manual editorial criterion recorded in review notes; no automated score gate or new schema field is claimed. Earlier informal chat scores do not retroactively certify every existing article or constitute Owner acceptance of a reference candidate. Apply the target at its next review; finance remains the next pilot.

Scope clarification, 30 October 2026: the author-and-independent-reviewer 9+ requirement and initial-plus-three-corrections limit apply to ongoing manual editorial work too. They do not expire with the trial. Only the dated task/runtime authorisation expires; topic corrections retain their separate two-round limit.
